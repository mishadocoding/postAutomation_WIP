# Circuit Market — remake directions

Status: new creative direction awaiting the user's selection. Preserve instagram/ and its exported MP4 as version 1. The user requests attention-grabbing art, music, coherent animation and less app-process demonstration, with examples possibly coming later. Keep 1080 × 1920 portrait for Instagram.

## Assumptions shared by all directions

20–25 seconds, 30 fps, music-led and intelligible muted, no voiceover. Build original editable vector illustrations with designed silhouettes, one stroke system and physically connected props. No generated bitmap artwork, stock dashboard illustration, gratuitous particles or borrowed campaign assets. The subject is a local ERP demo: no invented production capabilities or performance claims. Show a short verified product action after the visual premise is established, retaining real labels and values.

Music must be selected before final timing. Catalog auth currently reports not signed in; no project/global music candidates were found. The user has been asked to sign in or provide their own file. Do not omit music from the remake or fabricate a soundtrack in code. No paid generation has been selected.

## 1. Receipt avalanche — recommended

Why: a recognizable retail problem becomes a visual joke with a clear release of tension.
Device: a small shop under a mountain of busywork. Form: a continuous illustrated stage that changes scale, with receipt-to-screen match cuts.

We open inside a receipt printer that spits a strip directly toward the viewer. Pull back: the strip winds around a small shop, catching a box and tugging its awning; the owner braces against it with a visible reaction. At the music drop, the same receipt folds into a disciplined green path. It carries an actual demo order through a brief product detail, then returns as a tidy sale ticket; the owner gets their counter back. The last receipt folds into the end card.

First two seconds: at frame 0, a huge serrated receipt edge and a recognizable printer mouth fill the center of the portrait canvas; the receipt shoots forward and turns into the shop's runaway paper trail. Opening copy: “Running a shop?” No logo-only opening or empty entrance frame.

Beats: printer burst → paper tangles the shop → owner catches the strip → music drop / paper folds → brief real order and stock proof → clear counter / completed sale → receipt becomes brand end card.

Look: original shop illustration, bold black ink contours and two flat brand-derived colors on white; fold shadows are flat shapes that move with the paper, not generic soft UI shadows. Awning, counter, printer, owner and receipt share one perspective and stroke weight. Type behaves like printed shop signage, with a font chosen for that material; no default Inter campaign styling.

Sound: tense percussive opening, short break before the fold, then a punchy instrumental drop. Paper feed, tension snap and counter thump land on visible causes. Select an actual licensed track before setting shot frame ranges.

One thing to remember: run the shop with orders and stock connected.

## 2. Retail pinball

Device: one order as a physical carrier. Form: a camera journey through one constructed machine.

A sale ticket strikes a hanging shop bell, and the bell releases an order token onto a miniature retail machine. The token rolls past stock, checkout and dispatch; each mechanism physically hands it to the next. A jam creates the opening tension. Circuit Market briefly shows the real connected action, then the machine runs correctly and deposits the ticket on the counter.

First two seconds: a large shop bell is already in frame; a paper ticket smacks it on the first hit, and the camera follows its swinging clapper into the machine.

Beats: bell strike → token / jam → one connected product action → machine releases → stock-to-dispatch chain → ticket lands / end card.

Look: original flat vector construction with tactile cardboard edges, stamped labels and pinned joints. Brand green is the one active path, with ink and white structural surfaces. Every contact point is mapped to a coordinate; tokens never teleport between scenes.

Sound: syncopated instrumental with mechanical ticks, one dramatic stop at the jam and a resolved rolling rhythm. All sound follows the machine's actions.

One thing to remember: one sale moves through the shop as one connected operation.

## 3. A living shop poster — bold alternative

Device: printed words become the business world. Form: typography-driven match cuts and paper tears, with hard changes in scale.

“RUN THE SHOP” fills the portrait frame like a printed storefront poster. A real vector-drawn hand tears through the word SHOP, revealing a tiny shop behind it; the torn letters become its awning, shelves and counter. “NOT THE CHAOS” gets crumpled and stamped away. A short real-product action supplies proof, then the scene reconstructs itself into a finished campaign poster.

First two seconds: “RUN THE SHOP” is fully readable at frame 0; a hand enters and tears one purposeful opening through the O on the first musical accent.

Beats: poster hook → tear into illustrated shop → paper letters create disorder → product proof → clear shop / rebuild poster → brand and CTA.

Look: original vector print art, oversized condensed display type, hard ink edges and controlled misregistration only on non-reading decoration. Exactly one paper stock and one contour system. No generic grunge overlay, emoji, arbitrary geometry or image-generator output.

Sound: an instrumental with a strong downbeat and short breaks for tear / crumple / stamp actions. Type holds still while being read; cuts follow the action rather than every drum hit.

One thing to remember: keep the business moving while the workspace organizes the work.
